# Getting started with OpusPages

## Install

1. Download the installer from your beta invitation and run it. It installs for your account only, so no admin rights are needed.
2. Beta builds are not code signed yet, so Windows may show "Windows protected your PC." Click **More info**, then **Run anyway**.

The first time OpusPages opens, a short tour shows the basics. **Take the tour** on the start screen replays it.

## Open a PDF

Click **Open PDF** (or press Ctrl+O), pick a recent PDF on the start screen, or drop a PDF on the window. OpusPages opens PDFs from anywhere on your PC and saves changes back to the same file, about 1.5 seconds after each change. The status in the top right shows Edited, Saving, or Saved.

To keep the original untouched, use **Save as** first (the download button in the top bar, or Ctrl+Shift+S). Give the copy a name, and it saves next to the original unless you choose another folder. To save only some pages, pick Selected pages or type a range like 1-3, 5.

## Read and find

Scroll through the pages, or click a thumbnail on the left to jump. Zoom with Ctrl and the mouse wheel, or the buttons on the right of the toolbar. Click the percentage to fit the page to the window. Press Ctrl+F to find text. Enter goes to the next match.

## Organize pages

1. Click a thumbnail to select a page. Ctrl+click or Shift+click selects several.
2. Drag thumbnails to reorder.
3. Use the toolbar or right-click a thumbnail to rotate, delete, or merge another PDF in after the current page. To save some pages as a new PDF, right-click them, or use Save as and pick the pages.

## Merge and combine PDFs

Click **Combine PDFs** on the start screen and pick two or more PDFs (hold Ctrl to pick several). The combined PDF is saved to `Documents\OpusPages` and opens right away. To add another PDF to the one you have open, click the merge button in the toolbar, or drop the PDF on the window. Its pages go after the current page.

## Fill in a form

If a PDF has form fields, they show with a light teal tint. Click and type, or pick a choice. Everything saves as standard form data, so the filled PDF works in Acrobat, Edge, Chrome, and Preview.

## Turn any PDF into a form

Many PDFs look like forms but cannot be filled in, such as a printed Google Form or a scanned paper form.

1. Click **Prepare form** in the toolbar.
2. Click **Find fields**. OpusPages finds the answer lines, checkbox rows, and ovals, and names each field after its question. Each answer line gets its own field so text sits on the line. When a question has several lines, typing flows onto the next line as one fills up, and Enter moves down a line.
3. Check the list on the right. Rename a field by editing its name, or remove one with the x.
4. To add a field yourself, pick a type (Text, Paragraph, Checkbox, Dropdown, or Date) and drag a box on the page.
5. Click **Make fillable**. The PDF now has real form fields that anyone can fill in, in any PDF reader.

## Sign

Pick the Sign tool (or press S). The first time, draw your signature and click **Use signature**. Then click on the page to place it. Drag to move it and drag its corner to resize. OpusPages remembers your signature on this PC.

## Mark up

1. **Highlight** and **Underline:** pick the tool, then select text.
2. **Pen:** draw freehand.
3. **Text box:** click where the text should go and type.
4. **Sticky note:** click to place a note, then type. Double-click a note to edit it.

Click any markup to select it. Drag to move it, or press Delete to remove it. Ctrl+Z undoes any change.

## Appearance

Click the palette button in the top bar, or **Appearance** on the start screen, to pick System, Light, or Dark and a two-color theme. Pages always stay white so documents look the way they print.
